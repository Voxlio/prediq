The record

Every prediction on this page was written down and committed to a public repository 24 hours or less before kickoff, before the result existed. It is then never edited. The only thing ever added to a stored prediction is the final score, and the only way to change one would be a commit that anybody can see.

The ✓ and ✗ marks are worked out on this page as you load it, from the stored probabilities and the stored score — they are not saved anywhere. That is deliberate: a saved verdict would ask you to trust that whoever saved it applied the rule honestly, whereas this one you can check. Open any file in data/, read the three probabilities, read the score, and see whether the mark agrees.

gave it is the probability the model put on the thing that actually happened, which is the number that says most about a result. A miss where the model had the outcome at 30% is a normal day for a probability; a miss where it had it at 4% is the model being wrong about the match. A hit at 31% is closer to luck than to skill, and it is marked the same as any other, because the mark alone was never the interesting part.

Counts appear from the first finished match. A percentage does not, until 50 matches have been settled under the current model — a hit rate off a dozen games mostly measures which dozen games they were. Aggregate accuracy, whether calls made at 70% land near 70%, and how the model compares to the bookmakers will go on a separate page once there is enough record to support them.

Predictions for matches still to come are on the fixtures page, the ratings behind them are on the ratings page, and how the model arrives at a number is on the method page.

Prediq publishes statistical model output for information. These are probabilities, not tips — a 70% call still loses three times in ten. Not betting advice. 18+.
